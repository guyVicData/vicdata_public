**Real new content this unlocks, not just a placeholder swap**: once the backend brief's new `academic_subject_headline` rollup and RPC land, KS4 subject-level Results stops being "no real figure exists yet" — wire in the real average point score the same way `academic_subject_family_headline` already feeds Row C at category level, via the new RPC. ## Part 2 — The single-subject deep-dive view

Guy's ask: "the more granular subject views... in the same style" and "both" when asked whether this means finishing the four-row layout or something richer. This part is the something-richer half.

**What makes a subject worth its own focused view, beyond what the four rows already show**: those four rows are deliberately compact (one bar per category/subject, so many can be compared at a glance) — a deep dive trades that breadth for depth on one subject at a time. Real content worth including, all from data that already exists or will exist once the backend brief lands:

- The same four metrics (Candidates, Candidates %-change, Results, Results %-change), school vs. comparison set — but as the SAME real components already built (`SubjectAreaBarChart`/`SubjectAreaDivergingBarChart`/whatever Part 1 adds for the comparison side), not rebuilt from scratch, just given more room and shown together rather than one-row-among-many.
- **A real grade distribution**, not just the single averaged figure the bar charts show — `SubjectEntry`/the new subject rollup's own underlying grade breakdown (9/8/7.../U for GCSE, A\*/A/B.../U for A-level) as a real stacked or grouped bar, school vs. comparison-set average grade profile. This is genuinely new content, not a restyle of something that exists — the existing `SubjectTable` shows entries/value-added rows but not a grade-by-grade distribution chart.
- **KS5's real value-added figure**, with its real confidence interval, shown properly (the existing `SubjectTable` already has this data — reuse it, framed the same honest way every other CI-bearing figure in this codebase already is, e.g. the summary-wordings doc's own Progress 8 sentence template).
- **A real trend line back to 2020/21** once the backend brief's historic promotion lands, not just first/last-year %-change — this is the one piece of content a deep dive can show that the compact bar-chart rows structurally can't (a trend line needs room a one-bar-per-subject chart doesn't have).
- The comparator-set individual-school selector, same as Part 1, but perhaps more useful here since there's room to also show a real ranked list of every comparator school's own figure for this one subject (not just the picked one) — a natural fit for the extra space a focused view has.

**Build this as a variant/extension of the existing four-row engine, not a parallel implementation.** `SubjectAreaSection.tsx`'s subject-mode data-building (`buildSubjectRows`, per the last build report) already computes per-subject rows; a deep-dive view for ONE subject is a strict subset of "every subject in this category" with more chart types layered on, not a different data shape. Concretely: whatever component renders the deep dive should take the same subject-row shape Part 1's subject mode already produces, for one subject instead of iterating over all of them.

## Part 3 — Navigation: how you get to a focused view

Guy explicitly asked for design work here, not just a spec of the obvious option — three real approaches considered, with reasoning, and a recommendation.

**Option A — click a bar, filter in place.** Clicking any bar in the existing four rows sets a `subject` filter and the whole Section 03 area re-renders scoped to that one subject, in place, no new UI surface. Cheapest to build (the existing `subject` dropdown/`SubjectTable` mechanism already does almost exactly this). Weakest as "a focused view," though — the surrounding page doesn't change to make room, so a genuinely richer deep dive (Part 2's grade-distribution chart, trend line, ranked comparator list) has nowhere good to go without the page feeling cluttered or requiring a lot of scrolling past content that's no longer relevant once you've drilled in.

**Option B — a dedicated route/page per subject** (e.g. `/schools/[urn]/academic/[stage]/subjects/[subject]`). The most "focused" in the literal sense — a genuine destination, shareable/bookmarkable URL, full-page room for every Part 2 element without competing for space. Heaviest to build: new routing, its own loading/error states, a real "how did I get here / how do I get back" question to solve (breadcrumb or back-button reliance), and this product's existing pattern is emphatically NOT page-per-drill-down anywhere else (Map/Graphs/Rankings are views within one page, comparator sets are sidebar state, not routes) — a new page here would be the first time this app leaves that pattern for Academic Results specifically, worth being a deliberate choice if made, not a default.

**Option C — a breadcrumb-oriented slide-over/drawer panel. Recommended.** Clicking a bar or a subject name opens a drawer (full-height panel sliding in from the side, or a large centred overlay — implementation's call, but NOT the existing `FullscreenChartModal`, which is built for "one chart, bigger," not "a different, richer view") containing Part 2's full content. A breadcrumb strip at its top ("Whole school › Sciences & Maths › Chemistry") does two jobs at once: orientation (which level you're looking at) and navigation (click "Sciences & Maths" to go back to category level without fully closing the drawer, click "Whole school" to close it). Closing the drawer returns you to exactly where you were on the page — no lost scroll position, no new URL, no loading state for a page that was never navigated away from.

**Why this is the recommendation over A and C's alternatives**: it gets Option B's real benefit (genuine dedicated space for richer content, a clear sense of "I am now looking at one specific thing") without its real cost (new routing infrastructure, a pattern this app doesn't use anywhere else) or its own new problem (losing your place on the page). It gets Option A's cheapness on the DATA side (same underlying subject-row data, same four chart components, no new fetch shape beyond what Part 1 already builds) while giving Part 2's genuinely new content (grade distribution, trend line, ranked list) proper room, which Option A's in-place filtering structurally can't. The breadcrumb is what makes this feel different from "just a bigger modal" — it's a real drill-path, not a bigger version of the existing fullscreen-expand.

**How this reaches the drawer**: any bar across all four rows (both category mode's per-category bars and, once Part 1 lands, subject mode's per-subject bars) becomes a real click target opening the drawer scoped to that item — a category bar opens the drawer at category-with-subjects-listed (effectively today's subject-mode Section 03 content, but in the drawer instead of inline), and a subject bar (from subject mode, or from inside the drawer's own subject list) opens straight to Part 2's single-subject content. This makes the breadcrumb meaningful at both levels, not just the final one.
